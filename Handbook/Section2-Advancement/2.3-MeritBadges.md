# 2.3 Merit Badges

## What is a Merit Badge?

https://www.scouting.org/skills/merit-badges/

Merit badges recognize specific skills and achievements in a wide range of areas. Each badge represents completion of a series of requirements focused on a particular skill or knowledge area.

## How to Earn a Merit Badge

### Step 1: Choose Your Badge
Select a merit badge that interests you from the official list (100+ badges available).


### Step 2: Get Your Blue Card
- Obtain a blue card (officially called a "Merit Badge Pamphlet") from your Scoutmaster
- This tracks your progress and counselor's approval
- The card must be signed by both you and the counselor

### Step 3: Find a Merit Badge Counselor
- Merit badge counselors are trained adults who specialize in specific badges
- Your Scoutmaster can help connect you with a counselor
- You work one-on-one or in small groups with the counselor

### Step 4: Complete Requirements
- Work through all requirements with your counselor's guidance
- This typically takes several weeks
- Some badges require outdoor skills, others focus on career exploration
- The Merit Badge counselor is the only one who can mark a badge as complete. 

### Step 6: Receive Your Badge
- Once all requirements are approved, you have earned that badge. 
- badges are awarded at the next Court of Honor
- Badges should be proudly sewn onto the Merit Badge Sash to worn proudly

## Required Merit Badges for Eagle

https://www.scouting.org/skills/merit-badges/eagle-required/

To earn the Eagle rank, a minimum of 21 merit badge must be earned. 
Of this 21, there are 13 defined badges that must be earned ("Eagle Required"). Beyond this 13, 8 additional badges must be earned ("electives").

## Elective Merit Badges

Choose from 100+ additional badges in areas like:
- STEM and Technology
- Outdoor Skills
- Trades and Professions
- Sports and Recreation
- Arts and Music
- Environmental Conservation

# Q/A:

## Can I begin working on a merit badge before talking to my Scoutmaster and Merit Badge Counselor?
Generally - No. 

Work on a badge should not begin until the Scout has reached out to the Counselor to ensure that expectations are well understood.


**Key Points:**
- Merit badges showcase your diverse interests and skills
- Choose badges that genuinely interest you
- The journey is more important than just collecting badges
