# 2.1 Path to Eagle

## Rank Overview

The journey from Scout to Eagle typically takes 3-4 years of dedication and growth. The Eagle Rank is intended to represent the countless hours of committment the scout has undertaken to better themselves and those around them. 

Advacement is the responsibility of the Scout. Patrol members, and family members are encouraged to assist and encourage the Scout through advancement. Parents need to be mindful in ensuring that the Scout is completing the requirements themselves. Ultimately the Scout needs to earn the rank or merit badge, not the Parent(s).

### Ranks in Order:
1. **Scout** - Entry rank
2. **Tenderfoot** - Building foundational skills
3. **Second Class** - Outdoor skills development
4. **First Class** - Outdoor proficiency
5. **Star** - Leadership and service begin
6. **Life** - Advanced leadership and merit badges
7. **Eagle** - The pinnacle of achievement

## Requirements Summary

The offical advancement guides can be found on the Scouting.org website: https://www.scouting.org/programs/scouts-bsa/advancement-and-awards/

Additionally, Scouts can track their progress at https://advancement.scouting.org
### General Progression Path:
- Advancement requirements (skills, camping nights, merit badges)
- Service hour requirements increase with rank
- Leadership experience becomes more important at higher ranks
- Merit badge requirements: 21+ badges to reach Eagle

## Timeline Expectations

- Most scouts advance 1-2 ranks per year with active participation
- Scoutmaster conferences required for each rank
- Board of Review required for every rank after Scout

## Completing Requirements:

Scout rank through First Class, can have their requirements signed off by a Scout First Class or above (as designated by the Scoutmaster). Star, Life, and Eagle will be signed off by the Scoutmaster (but tracked in the book by the Scout). 

Requirements are considered complete when the Scout has completed exactly what the requirements says (no more, no less). The Scout can not "fail a requirement" because they have poor understanding of the intended skill. Ex: Inability to remeber how to tie a particular knot, does not necessarily mean that you hadn't tied that knot previously. 

Scouts may be asked during a Scoutmaster Conference or Board of Review to review their experience of that requirement. This may include a request to repeat the requirement. Inability to complete the task during the SM Conference or Board of Review, is not grounds to withold advancement.

---

**Key Points:**
- Advancement is individual - scouts progress at their own pace
- Leadership and service are key to advancement
- Persistence and effort are rewarded
