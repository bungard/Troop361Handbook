# 3.4 Camping & Hiking Rules

## The Buddy System

### Rule:
**Never go anywhere alone - always have a buddy or group of at least 2 scouts together.**

### Why It Matters:
- Safety - someone can help if an emergency occurs
- Accountability - leadership knows where everyone is
- Support - hiking and camping is more fun with friends
- First aid - injured scout can get help immediately

### How It Works:
- Pairs or small groups stay together at all times
- Check in with patrol leader before moving to different areas
- If separated, return to last known point or nearest adult
- Groups should not go beyond adult sight lines

## Campsite Setup

### Choosing a Campsite:
- [ ] Use designated campsites when available
- [ ] Choose durable ground (rock, gravel, established sites)
- [ ] Avoid vegetation and soft soil
- [ ] Stay 200 feet from water sources
- [ ] Ensure drainage (no low spots that collect water)
- [ ] Close to firewood sources (if fires are allowed)
- [ ] Open to breeze (helps with smoke and insects)

### Setting Up Camp:
1. Organize gear in an orderly fashion
2. Set up tents properly (vestibule facing away from others)
3. Establish a cooking/eating area away from sleeping area
4. Set up latrine area 200 feet from water and camp
5. Create storage area for backpacks
6. Mark any hazards or trip hazards

### Campsite Etiquette:
- Keep noise levels reasonable, especially at night
- Respect other groups' privacy and space
- Clean up after meals immediately
- Don't wander through others' campsites

## Weather Safety

### Before the Trip:
- Check weather forecast
- Prepare for worst possible conditions
- Bring layers for temperature changes
- Have rain gear regardless of forecast

### In Cold Weather:
- Dress in layers that you can add/remove
- Keep dry - change wet clothing immediately
- Eat enough calories for energy and warmth
- Watch for signs of hypothermia
- Stay active but don't overexert yourself

### In Rain:
- Set up camp on higher ground for drainage
- Ensure tent is waterproof and properly ventilated
- Keep sleeping bag inside tent and dry
- Use rain gear on trails
- Cross streams carefully - swift water is dangerous

### In Hot Weather:
- Drink plenty of water throughout the day
- Take breaks during hottest hours if possible
- Wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothes
- Use sunscreen and hat
- Watch for signs of heat exhaustion
- Electrolyte drinks help with longer hikes

### Lightning Safety:
- Avoid open areas and tall trees during storms
- Move to lower ground or forested area
- Stay out of water
- Don't use electronics during thunderstorms
- Wait at least 30 minutes after last thunder before resuming activities

## Hiking Safely

### Before You Hike:
- [ ] Tell adult exactly where you're going
- [ ] Check weather
- [ ] Bring all Ten Essentials
- [ ] Wear proper footwear
- [ ] Bring adequate water and snacks
- [ ] Start early enough to finish before dark
- [ ] Know your group's fitness level

### During the Hike:
- Stay on marked trails
- Hike at the pace of the slowest person
- Take regular breaks
- Drink water frequently (don't wait until thirsty)
- Watch for hazards (loose rocks, steep drop-offs, etc.)
- Use buddy system at all times
- Report all injuries to adult leader immediately

### Trail Hazards:
- Loose rocks and roots - watch your footing
- Slippery wet surfaces - move slowly and carefully
- Steep terrain - use switchbacks instead of straight down
- Swift water crossings - scout the crossing first
- Insects and animals - keep distance, don't approach

### If Someone Gets Injured:
1. Stop and assess the injury
2. Alert adult leader immediately
3. Provide first aid if trained
4. Determine if hike can continue safely
5. Modify route if necessary
6. Report to parents/guardians

---

**Key Points:**
- Preparation prevents most problems
- Communication keeps everyone safe
- Weather can change quickly - be ready
- Buddy system is non-negotiable
