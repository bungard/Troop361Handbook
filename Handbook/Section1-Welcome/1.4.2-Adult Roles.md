# 1.4.2 Troop Organization - Adult Roles

## Adult Key 3 
This is made of the Scoutmaster, Committee Chair, and Chartered Org Representative. These indviduals serve different roles to help the troop be successful. While it may not seem like it at times, these individuals do not have a hierarchy amongst themselves or over the Youth Leadership. Collectively, they work together to ensure the success of the program. 


### Chartered Org Representative (COR)

The chartered organization representative is the direct contact between the troop and the chartered organization and is a member of the troop’s Key 3. The chartered organization rep. is also the liaison to the district committee and the local council.
- Help select the right leadership for the troop.
- Serves as a liaison between the troop and the organization.
- Helps with the charter renewal.
- Cultivates organization leaders.
- Cultivates resource to support the organization.

### Scout Master (SM)

The Scoutmaster is responsible for the image and program of the troop. The Scoutmaster and assistant Scoutmasters work directly with the Scouts.
- Train and guide youth leaders.
- Work with responsible adults to bring Scouting to
boys.
- Meet regularly with the patrol leaders’ council.
- Attend all troop meetings, and when necessary
arrange a qualified adult substitute.
- Conduct parent sessions to share the program and
encourage parent participation.
- Conduct Scoutmaster conferences for all ranks.
- Delegate responsibilities to other adult members
so that they are fully involved.
- Participate in council and district events.
- Conduct all activities under qualified leadership,
safe conditions, and the policies of the BSA and
the chartered organization.
- Report to the troop committee at each meeting.

#### Assistant Scout Master (ASM)

The Assistant Scoutmaster is responsible in supporting and assisting the Scoutmaster and filling in when needed. The Assistant Scoutmaster also works directly with the Scouts.
- Helps new scouts adjust to the troop.
- Assists with training and skills instruction.
- Assists both scout and adult with Quartermaster duties.


### Committee Chair

The committee Chair is responsible for 
- Organize the committee to see that all functions are delegated, coordinated, and completed.
- Maintain a close relationship with the chartered organization rep. and the Scoutmaster.
- Interpret national and local policies to the troop.
- Prepare troop committee meeting agendas.
- Call, preside over and promote attendance at monthly troop committee meetings.
- Ensure troop representation at monthly roundtable meetings.

The committee chair is elected by the Troop committee on an annual basis. 

#### The Committee

Troop committee is an organized group of registered adults of the troop. To become a “Registeredadult” you are required to maintain an active membership with Scouting America, maintain an active [Safeguarding Youth Training](https://www.gtcscouting.org/Safety/youth-protection). Each family is encouraged to have at least one parent on the Troop committee.

After one month of membership and, having attended one Troop committee meeting, every member is
then eligible to vote.

A number of key roles exist on the Committee to help support the Troop functions. 
These include (but aren't limited to):
- Treasurer
- Fundraising Chair
- Outdoor Activities Chair
- Eagle Advisor
- Training / Membership Chair
- Adult Quartermaster
