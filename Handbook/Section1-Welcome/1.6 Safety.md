# 1.6 Safety

Safety is a core responsibility of every scout, leader, and parent in Troop 361.
No activity, advancement goal, or schedule pressure ever outweighs the well-being of our scouts.

## The Guide to Safe Scouting

All troop activities are governed by Scouting America's **Guide to Safe Scouting (GSS)**:

> **[Guide to Safe Scouting — scouting.org/health-and-safety/gss/](https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/gss/)**

The GSS is the authoritative reference for what is and is not permitted in Scouting. When a question about activity safety arises — whether it involves camping, transportation, aquatics, climbing, or any other program area — the GSS is the first place to look. Leaders are expected to know it; scouts and parents are encouraged to read it.

## Key Safety Principles

- **Two-deep leadership** — A minimum of two registered adult leaders must be present at every activity.
- **Buddy system** — Scouts never go anywhere alone. Pairs or groups of three are required.
- **Qualified supervision** — Activities requiring specific training (aquatics, climbing, shooting sports) are led only by adults with the appropriate certifications.
- **Youth protection** — All adult leaders complete Scouting America's Youth Protection Training before working with scouts. See section [4.2](../Section4-Operations/4.2-CodeOfConduct.md) for full details.
- **Medical forms** — Every scout must have a current Annual Health and Medical Record on file with the troop before participating in any overnight or high-adventure activity.

## When Something Feels Unsafe

If any scout, parent, or leader believes an activity is unsafe:
1. Speak up immediately — safety concerns are never dismissed.
2. The activity stops until the concern is resolved.
3. Leaders consult the GSS or contact the Greate Trail Council if clarification is needed.

There is no rank requirement, no campout, and no merit badge worth a preventable injury.
